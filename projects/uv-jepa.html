UV-JEPA

UV-JEPA is an ongoing final-year research project investigating whether latent-predictive vision-language architectures can learn effective multimodal representations in low-resource language regimes—using Urdu as the representative benchmark—with high data efficiency and without conventional autoregressive decoding.

Overview & Research Motivation

Vision-language learning has increasingly evolved from token-level autoregressive generation or pixel reconstruction toward learning shared, continuous representations in latent space. Modern Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (such as I-JEPA, VL-JEPA, and TC-JEPA) demonstrate that predicting representations rather than decoding discrete tokens reduces error compounding and improves representation quality.

However, most multimodal foundation models are benchmarked almost exclusively on high-resource English datasets with tens of millions of curated image-text pairs. When applied to low-resource languages, standard models struggle due to severe data scarcity, morphological richness, and caption variance.

UV-JEPA investigates whether latent-predictive vision-language learning remains effective in low-resource settings, using Urdu as the primary testbed. The central hypothesis is that predicting a compact sentence representation from an image representation in latent space provides competitive vision-language alignment while requiring substantially less linguistic supervision than conventional autoregressive caption generation.

Methodology & Architecture

The UV-JEPA framework formulates vision-language learning as a non-generative, latent-predictive task:

  • Vision & Language Encoders: Uses a Vision Transformer (ViT-Small) for visual feature extraction alongside a pretrained language encoder (based on urduhack/roberta-urdu-small) to process target text descriptions.
  • EMA Target Text Encoder: The target text representation is generated by an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) encoder with stop-gradient, while the online text encoder updates jointly to provide the source for the target representation.
  • Lightweight Cross-Attention Predictor: Maps visual representations into the shared latent space and is trained to predict the corresponding continuous caption representation using normalized latent regression.
  • Rigorous Baseline Benchmarks: UV-JEPA is evaluated directly against rigorously tuned contrastive (CLIP-style) and autoregressive decoders under identical, constrained data regimes to measure data efficiency.

Evaluation & Empirical Focus

The project's principal contribution is an empirical study evaluating whether latent prediction offers measurable advantages when paired training data is scarce:

  • Cross-Modal Retrieval: Bi-directional image-to-text and text-to-image retrieval performance across multiple data scales.
  • Representation Quality & Probing: Semantic caption similarity, feature stability, and probing of learned latent spaces.
  • Data Efficiency & Computational Cost: Benchmarking scaling behavior, parameter efficiency, and training overhead against baselines.
  • Robustness: Evaluating sensitivity to caption quality variations and linguistic noise inherent to low-resource web collections.
